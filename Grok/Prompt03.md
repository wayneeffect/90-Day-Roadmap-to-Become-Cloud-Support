**Most Cloud Support roles (Cloud Support Engineer, Associate, or Specialist) prefer or require a bachelor’s degree in a related field, but it is not always strictly mandatory—especially at the entry level.** Practical experience, industry certifications, and demonstrated skills frequently serve as strong substitutes.

### Typical Education Requirements
- **Preferred / Common**: Bachelor’s degree in Computer Science, Information Technology (IT), Software Engineering, Networking, Information Systems, or another STEM-related field. Coursework in systems administration, networking, databases, security, and cloud concepts is beneficial.
- **Acceptable alternatives**:
  - Associate’s degree in a technical field + relevant experience (sometimes several years).
  - Equivalent professional experience in IT support, systems administration, or technical troubleshooting (many employers explicitly accept this in place of a degree).
  - High school diploma or GED combined with strong certifications and hands-on skills for some entry-level associate or specialist positions.
- **Advanced roles**: Senior or principal positions more often list a bachelor’s (or master’s preferred) plus extensive experience. Some leadership tracks may favor a master’s in CS, IT, or related fields.

### Variations by Role Level
| Role Level                  | Typical Education Expectation                          | Notes |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|-------|
| Cloud Support Associate / Entry-level | Bachelor’s preferred; associate’s degree, certifications, or equivalent experience often accepted | Lowest barrier; AWS programs note no prior degree required |
| Cloud Support Engineer (mid-level) | Bachelor’s commonly preferred or required            | Experience + certifications can outweigh formal degree |
| Senior / Specialist / Principal | Bachelor’s (Master’s preferred in some cases) + 5–10+ years experience | Degree more consistently listed |

### Importance of Certifications and Experience
Formal education provides a foundation, but **cloud provider certifications and hands-on experience are often more decisive** for hiring:

- **Foundational (highly recommended for entry)**: AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner, Microsoft Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900), Google Cloud Digital Leader.
- **Associate-level**: AWS Solutions Architect / SysOps / Developer Associate, Azure Administrator Associate (AZ-104), Google Associate Cloud Engineer.
- Practical experience via IT helpdesk/support roles, internships, home labs, open-source contributions, or bootcamps/apprenticeships is highly valued.
- Soft skills (communication, customer service, troubleshooting) and technical basics (Linux, networking, scripting in Python/Bash) matter significantly.

Many sources note that employers increasingly hire based on demonstrable skills rather than diplomas alone. Career changers without a traditional CS degree can succeed by building a portfolio of labs/projects, earning certifications, and starting in support roles.

### Bottom Line
A related **bachelor’s degree is the most common listed requirement** and provides a competitive edge, but it is not an absolute barrier. Entry-level Cloud Support Associate positions are among the more accessible cloud roles for those with certifications, IT support experience, or targeted training programs (such as the AWS Cloud Support Associate Professional Certificate, which requires no prior degree). Always check specific job postings, as requirements vary by employer (e.g., Big Tech, MSPs, government contractors) and location.
